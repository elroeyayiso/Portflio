01 — ABOUT ME

Technology became
meaningful to me
through people.

I'm Elroe, a data scientist and researcher interested in how technology can help people understand information, access opportunities, and solve problems that affect their everyday lives.

My interest in technology comes from both curiosity and experience. Growing up in Ethiopia, I saw how limited access to technology and information could create barriers for people and communities.

02
WHY TECHNOLOGY

I wanted to understand how technology could close gaps.

As I learned more about technology, I became interested in more than just building software. I wanted to understand who technology was being built for, who was being left out, and how data could help us better understand people's experiences.

That perspective has shaped the projects and research I have chosen throughout college and beyond. Whether I am working with data, building an application, or conducting research, I try to keep the people affected by the technology at the center of the work.

03 — COMMUNITY

Technology should create more opportunities.

01

Teaching students

Through AVELA XR & AI Lab, I worked with students who had limited access to technology and taught programming, data, and XR concepts. Working with students from communities that are often underrepresented in technology reminded me that access and exposure can make a major difference in whether someone sees technology as something they can participate in.

02

Working with immigrant communities

My research at the University of Washington focused on understanding how Ethiopian immigrant communities interact with health information and data. Through interviews, workshops, and diary studies, I learned how language, culture, and existing support systems can influence how people understand and use information.

03

Looking beyond the data

These experiences taught me that a technical solution is only part of the process. Understanding the people, communities, and circumstances behind the data is just as important.

04 — RESEARCH

WHAT I LEARNED

Data tells a story, but people give it meaning.

My research experiences have allowed me to work directly with communities and explore how technology can better reflect their needs.

From studying public transportation waiting times in Addis Ababa to exploring how immigrant communities understand health data, I have become interested in the space between data and lived experience.

That is one of the reasons I enjoy data science and research. The technical side gives me tools to find patterns, while research helps me understand what those patterns actually mean.
